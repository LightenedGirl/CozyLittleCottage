Different Types of Solar Inverters

May 20, 2026

There are several types of solar inverters used in homes, and each one is designed for different system sizes, budgets, and backup needs.

The inverter is the heart of a home solar system — it converts solar energy into usable electricity for everyday appliances.

1. String Inverter

A string inverter connects multiple solar panels together in a “string.”

  • How It Works: Panels connect in series and one inverter converts DC to AC for the whole system.
  • Advantages: Affordable, reliable, and easy to maintain.
  • Disadvantages: One shaded panel can reduce the output of the entire string.
  • Best For: Homes with simple roofs and little shading.
Popular Brands

SMA Solar Technology · Growatt · Sungrow

2. Microinverter

A microinverter is installed behind each individual solar panel.

  • Advantages: Better performance in shaded conditions.
  • Monitoring: Each panel works independently.
  • Disadvantages: Higher installation cost.
  • Best For: Roofs with shade or multiple roof directions.
Popular Brands

Enphase Energy · APsystems

3. Hybrid Inverter

Hybrid inverters work with solar panels, batteries, and grid power simultaneously.

  • Advantages: Battery backup support and smart energy management.
  • Useful During: Power cuts and unstable electricity supply.
  • Disadvantages: More expensive and slightly complex.
  • Best For: Homes needing reliable backup power.
Popular Brands

Luminous · UTL Solar · Victron Energy

4. Off-Grid Inverter

Off-grid inverters are designed for homes completely independent from the utility grid.

  • Requires batteries for energy storage.
  • Works without a utility connection.
  • Useful for remote locations and villages.
  • Needs careful power planning and larger battery banks.

5. Grid-Tied Inverter

Grid-tied inverters are made for systems connected directly to the utility grid.

  • No batteries required.
  • Extra solar power can be exported to the grid.
  • Lower installation cost and high efficiency.
  • Stops working during power cuts for safety reasons.
Best For

Urban homes with stable electricity and net metering.

Pure Sine Wave vs Modified Sine Wave

Pure Sine Wave Inverter

Produces cleaner and smoother electricity that is safer for sensitive appliances.

  • Best for TVs, refrigerators, laptops, and computers.
  • Preferred for modern homes.

Modified Sine Wave Inverter

Cheaper but less efficient and may create appliance issues.

  • Can cause heating and electrical noise.
  • Not ideal for sensitive electronics.

Pure sine wave inverters are strongly recommended for most modern homes.

Which Inverter Is Best for a Home?

Situation Best Inverter
Simple roof, low cost String inverter
Roof with shade Microinverter
Need battery backup Hybrid inverter
No grid connection Off-grid inverter
City home with net metering Grid-tied inverter

Common Inverter Mistakes

  • Buying undersized inverters
  • Ignoring battery compatibility
  • Installing in hot locations
  • Skipping surge protection
  • Poor ventilation
Important

Heat is one of the biggest inverter killers. Proper ventilation and installation location matter a lot.
