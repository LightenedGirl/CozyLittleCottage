The 6 things to check before you buy

Going solar isn't as simple as calling an installer and signing a cheque. There are a few things worth checking first — and getting them right makes a real difference to how well your system performs and how quickly it pays back.

1 First Things First

Is your roof ready?
Check the direction (south-facing is best), angle (30–45° is ideal), any shade from trees or nearby buildings, and the age of your roof. If your roof needs replacing in the next few years — do that first. You don't want to uninstall panels for a roof job.

2 Size It Right

How many panels do you need?
A typical home needs 6–12 panels depending on how much electricity you use. Check your electricity bill for your monthly kWh figure — that number is the starting point for calculating the right system size. Most installers will do this calculation for you for free.

3 Know Your Options

Types of solar panels
Monocrystalline — most efficient, sleek black appearance, best for limited roof space. Polycrystalline — blue tint, slightly less efficient, budget-friendlier. Thin-film — flexible and lightweight, but less efficient. Most homes go monocrystalline.

4 Budget

What does it actually cost?
In India, a basic 3kW home system costs roughly ₹1.5–2.5 lakhs before subsidies. The central government's PM Surya Ghar scheme offers subsidies up to ₹78,000 for qualifying homes. Always check what your state offers too —
Kerala has additional KSEB incentives.

5 Optional but Useful

Do you need a battery?
Not essential — but a home battery stores surplus solar energy for use at night or cloudy days, making you far more self-sufficient. It adds to the upfront cost but reduces dependence on the grid significantly. Worth considering if there are frequent power cuts in your area.

6 Final Step

Finding a good installer
Always get at least 3 quotes. Check that the installer is MNRE-empanelled (for government subsidies). Ask about warranties — panels should carry 25 years, inverter 5–10 years. Be wary of deals that seem too cheap.

If you're in Kerala
Kerala gets excellent solar irradiation year-round — even during the monsoon, panels still generate meaningful power. KSEB offers a net metering scheme, which means any surplus electricity you generate goes back to the grid and is credited against your bill. It's one of the better state schemes in India.
